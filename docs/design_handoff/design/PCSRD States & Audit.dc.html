17 — AUDIT & RECOMMENDATIONS DESIGN vs THE PLAN DOCUMENTS
17.1

Product understanding

A bilingual institutional website for a licensed Palestinian civil-society organisation working across the five governorates of the Gaza Strip, with three purposes in a stated priority order: pass institutional donor due diligence (the primary conversion is a partnership inquiry, never a donation), protect the community from impersonation, and let non-technical Arabic-speaking staff publish. There is no payment path anywhere by design; support is a WhatsApp deep link. The design follows that order of priority literally — which is why partnership and channel verification are the only two actions in persistent chrome.

Institutional reader

Donor, UN agency, INGO compliance officer. Needs licence, governance, verifiable figures, partners, policies — fast, in English, from a shared link.

Community reader

Arabic, on a phone, often on a poor connection. Needs eligibility, how to access a service, and certainty about which channel is genuinely the organisation's.

Staff author

Arabic-first, non-technical, publishing 2–4 items a month. Needs one form shape, both locales visible at once, and blocks that stop protection mistakes before they publish.

17.6

Recommendations, in the order they should be taken

CRITICAL — BLOCKS THE BUILD
Real organisational facts

Problem: legal name, licence number, licensing authority, founding year, office hours and the strategic plan text are bracketed placeholders throughout. Evidence: every frame in every file. Why it matters: the identity ledger is the due-diligence surface; with placeholders it cannot be reviewed for accuracy, only for layout. Do: supply the strategic plan and registration documents; I load them into the frames before implementation starts. Impact: turns a layout review into a content review.

Confirm the official channels

Problem: /verify lists one real phone number from the environment file and placeholders for the rest, including a “former account, no longer used” row that is invented structure. Why it matters: a wrong or incomplete channel list on this page is worse than no page — it can legitimise an impersonator. Do: give me the complete, verified list including any accounts that should be publicly marked as retired. Impact: the page becomes usable as designed.

Token sign-off

Problem: three colour tokens depart from 04-DESIGN-SYSTEM §1. Why it matters: tokens are the one thing that must be settled before any component is written; changing them later is a repaint of everything. Do: accept the warm set, or reject it and I revert to white / #F6F7FB / #DDE3EE across all files in one pass. Impact: unblocks M1.

HIGH — SIGNIFICANT UX OR ENGINEERING IMPACT
Field photography

Twenty-eight image slots sit empty. The design treats photography as captioned evidence with a credit and a consent reference — it needs real images with those attributes, or the caption apparatus is decoration. Supply 8–12 images with activity, governorate, date and consent status per image.

Governorate map geometry

Decide now whether the five governorates are drawn as real geometry (accurate boundaries, one static SVG, ~4KB) or stay as the ordered rows. Both are defensible; the rows are more legible on a phone, the map is more credible to a donor. This affects the home and projects layouts, so decide before M3.

Ten remaining public routes

News list and detail, programmes index, partners, memberships, get-involved index, volunteer form, support page, resources, legal pages. Each follows a pattern already drawn — roughly one further pass. The volunteer form and support page are the two with genuinely new content and should be drawn, not inferred.

Media library and consent workflow

The consent gate is drawn as a blocking error at the point of publish, but the screen where a staff member resolves it — the library detail panel with alt text, credit, consent reference and the identifiable-minors flag — is not. It is the second-most protection-critical screen in the admin after the confidential inbox.

MEDIUM — MEANINGFUL IMPROVEMENTS
◆OG image templates. Social sharing is the organisation's dominant distribution channel and per-template OG cards are specified, but none are designed. Four templates cover everything: project, story, post, vacancy.
◆Arabic numeral decision. The design uses Latin digits inside Arabic throughout, per the current formatter setting. It is a one-line change to Eastern Arabic numerals and it materially changes the look of every figure — settle it while it is still one line.
◆Nine remaining admin entity forms. Field configs against 14.1, but metrics and partners each have a field type worth drawing once: the metric period/status pair, and the logo-permission control.
◆Print styling for the dossier pages. Compliance officers print. About, governance and a project record should have a print stylesheet; the ruled, shadow-free design already prints well.
◆Admin on a tablet. The spec exempts the admin from mobile, but staff will open the inbox on a phone. A one-column fallback for the inbox and the status panel is cheap insurance.
LOW — POLISH
◆A copy-to-clipboard confirmation on /verify (the copy buttons have no success feedback drawn).
◆Favicon and app-manifest icon set from the logo mark, cropped to the wheat-and-figure device without the wordmark.
◆An RSS-discovery link and a newsletter question — currently no subscription surface exists at all, which may be intentional.
◆Focus-visible specimen: the 3px gold ring is specified and used, but not shown in the foundations plate.
